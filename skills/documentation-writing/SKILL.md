# Documentation Writing

Make the document help a specific reader understand, decide, or act.

- Establish the reader, desired outcome, deliverable, source of truth, and
  constraints before choosing a structure.
- Inspect authoritative material before drafting. Keep factual claims,
  recommendations, assumptions, and unresolved gaps distinguishable.
- Organize around the reader's task or decision rather than the order in which
  information was discovered.
- Preserve requested terminology, voice, format, and existing repository
  conventions. Do not broaden the document's scope without a concrete need.
- Use examples only when they clarify behavior; make commands, paths, inputs,
  outputs, and prerequisites exact enough to follow.
- Prefer direct language, informative headings, and short transitions. Remove
  repetition that does not improve navigation or understanding.
- Before delivery, check factual support, completeness, consistency, links,
  examples, and the next action expected from the reader.

When editing repository documentation, inspect adjacent documents and the
implementation they describe. Validate links and executable examples when
practical. Never invent product behavior to make a document feel complete;
surface the missing fact or decision instead.
